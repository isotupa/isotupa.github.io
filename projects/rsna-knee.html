Kaggle · RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection

Twelve findings from one knee MRI

Each study is a set of MRI series acquired in one session, and the task is to give it twelve probabilities. Almost none of the work that mattered was in the network.

Role
Solo entry. Label extraction, DICOM pipeline, model and validation design.
Stack
PyTorch, pydicom, DINOv2, NumPy, pandas
When
2026

What the score rewards, and what that removes

The score is the unweighted mean of twelve per-label ROC AUCs — anterior cruciate and medial collateral ligament injury, medial and lateral meniscal tear, osteoarthritis in each of three compartments, effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, bone contusion, fracture. Three consequences follow, and each one deletes a design decision rather than opening it.

Only order matters. AUC is invariant under any strictly increasing map of a label's scores, so calibration and thresholds are worth nothing. It also settles how to ensemble: averaging raw probabilities lets whichever model is most confident dominate, whereas averaging ranks combines the only information the metric reads. Every submission in this pipeline is written as per-column ranks, and the final file is a rank mean over 20 members.

Every label costs the same. A label left at chance contributes 0.5 instead of roughly the mean AUC a good model reaches, forfeiting about a twelfth of the gap however well the other eleven do. At a mean of 0.85 that is 0.029 of final score. Rare findings therefore deserve more attention than common ones, which is the opposite of where effort naturally goes.

Where the targets come from

Only a small subset of training studies carry the twelve per-condition labels. Every training study carries the original radiology report.

The decisive fact is in the schemas rather than the prose: train.csv has a Report column and test.csv does not. Text is available when fitting and absent when predicting. That rules out a fusion model with a text branch — at inference it would have nothing to read — and leaves the reports usable only as training targets, or as a weight on how confidently a study could be read. I use them as both.

Reading a report in nine languages

The reports arrive in English, Spanish, French, Dutch, German, Turkish, Croatian/Serbian, Greek and Bulgarian/Russian, and nothing says which. The obvious move is to identify the language and route to a per-language extractor. I did not, and the reason is that cheap identification fails badly: substring tests like 'la ' for French match Spanish just as often, and whichever test runs first swallows both. Committing to a guess before any evidence is read is worse than not guessing.

So every cue lexicon carries all nine languages at once and each clause is tested against the union. Greek and Cyrillic cues cannot collide with Latin-script ones, and the Latin-script vocabularies here are close enough that a shared cue is usually right. The price is paid in coverage rather than in precision.

Normalisation comes first, then segmentation, then scoping. Case, diacritics and separators are folded up front, which also repairs a real encoding problem: many Greek reports spell mu with the MICRO SIGN U+00B5 rather than U+03BC, and NFKD decomposition maps one onto the other. Turkish dotted and dotless i have to be folded before casefolding or İZLENMEZ and izlenmez diverge into two different words.

NEGATION  no · not · without · sin · ausencia · pas de · sans · geen
          keine · ohne · yok · izlenmemekte · nema · bez · δεν · χωρις · няма

ANAT/ACL  anterior cruciate · acl · cruzado anterior · lca · croise anterieur
          voorste kruisband · vkb · vorderes kreuzband · on capraz · ocb
          prednji krizni · χιαστ* · предна кръстна

Scope for a negator is the whole clause, not a character window. A window mis-scopes across languages with different word order — Turkish puts its negator at the end of the sentence and English at the front — and clause segmentation already keeps statements short enough that the whole clause is the right unit. Each match comes out as an assertion, a negation or a hedge, and the extractor emits a score together with a confidence.

What the scanner actually recorded

train_series.csv describes each series with an anatomical plane and two binary flags, Fluid_Sensitive and Fat_Suppression. The names denote two physically independent properties, and that independence is exactly what the delivered columns do not have: across the training series the two agree on every row. As given they carry one axis where there should be two.

Both are recoverable from the DICOM header. Fluid sensitivity is a property of the contrast weighting, set by repetition and echo time — T1 below roughly 800 ms TR, T2 above it with a long TE, proton density above it with a short one — with gradient echo breaking the rule and settled by ScanningSequence first. Fat suppression is a preparation applied on top of any weighting, and it is what makes marrow oedema conspicuous.

A bug that produces no error

A series is a directory of files, and the obvious way to walk it is to sort the filenames. That is wrong here, and wrong silently. The filename is the SOP Instance UID, assigned to be unique rather than ordered, so sorting by it yields a sequence uncorrelated with anatomy — measured on a series from this corpus, the rank correlation between filename order and physical position is about 0.01.

Three things downstream depend on that order. Three adjacent slices stacked as three channels become three unrelated cross-sections. The middle of the stack becomes a random subset. And reversing the order to normalise laterality does nothing at all.

The true order is recoverable exactly from geometry every slice already carries. ImageOrientationPatient gives the in-plane axes, ImagePositionPatient the position of the first voxel, and the projection of the position onto their cross product increases monotonically along the stack.

Two constraints on the pixels

Physical scale, not pixel count. A slice of N × N pixels with spacing s mm covers N·s millimetres of anatomy, and both vary across this corpus, so a fixed-pixel resize hands the encoder images whose physical scale differs by a factor of several. Cropping to a constant millimetre extent and then resampling to a fixed pixel count fixes the pitch instead.

A feature narrower than two pixels does not survive the resize. That is Nyquist applied to the resampling grid: representing a structure d mm wide requires an effective pitch of at most d/2. A meniscal tear is one to three millimetres, so at the low end the pitch has to be 0.5 mm or better — and if it is not, no amount of capacity downstream recovers the signal, because it was destroyed before the first convolution. This is a property of the resize, not of the network.

Normalising left and right

Four of the twelve targets are medial/lateral pairs and a fifth is named for the side it lies on. Medial and lateral are defined relative to the body's midline, so which side of the image they fall on depends on which knee was scanned. Left unnormalised, those five labels learn from an axis the model cannot observe.

The correction differs by plane. Coronally and axially the medial–lateral direction lies in the image plane, so flipping the last axis maps one knee onto the other. Sagittally it is the slice axis: each slice is unchanged by mirroring, and what differs is the direction in which the stack traverses the joint, so the slice order is reversed instead.

Laterality is a Type 2C attribute — it may legitimately be absent, and here it is absent on half the studies, by whole vendors rather than scattered series. Leaving those alone silently declares them left-sided, so every right knee among them enters the model mirrored. Recovering the side from the patient-space geometry is what makes the normalisation worth doing at all.

Aggregating sequences into twelve decisions

A study arrives as up to six slot embeddings with a presence mask. Pooling them identically would throw away the reason the protocol has three planes in the first place: cruciates are read sagittally, collateral ligaments and the meniscal body coronally, patellar cartilage axially. A mean over slots dilutes the one carrying the evidence with five that do not.

The head is deliberately this small. The label is attached to the study, so nothing in the supervision says which part of a study carries the evidence. Attention parameters below the slot level would have nothing to learn from and would spend their capacity fitting noise.

Why the encoder is trained rather than frozen

A frozen self-supervised encoder puts a ceiling on everything downstream. Resolution, encoder size, slice coverage and slot aggregation change how much the model looks and how closely, but none of them changes the vocabulary it looks with — and that ceiling should be expected to bind here, because the encoder learned its features from natural images, where nothing resembles the signal a torn meniscus makes on a proton-density sequence.

So the encoder is adapted, under two restraints. Only the last blocks move: early blocks of a vision transformer are generic edge and texture filters, late blocks are where semantics live, and there may not be enough supervision here to improve the early ones while there is certainly enough to damage them. And the encoder learns far more slowly than the head — the head is random at initialisation and has everything to learn, the encoder starts from a good solution and only needs moving off it. A single learning rate leaves the head untrained or destroys the encoder within a few hundred steps.

Augmentations I deliberately did not use

Neither flip is available, for different reasons. A horizontal flip would reintroduce the nuisance axis the laterality normalisation just removed — it undoes, once per batch, what the whole header pass exists to establish. A vertical flip is not a nuisance axis at all: a knee is acquired in a canonical orientation and no study here looks like its own vertical mirror. An augmentation is meant to cover directions along which the label does not change, and where a finding sits in the frame is information rather than noise — a Baker's cyst is identified by lying in the popliteal fossa, not by its appearance alone.

What is left is jitter no label depends on: a few degrees of rotation, a few per cent of scale and translation.

Validating without fooling yourself

Two leaks are specific to this setup, and both inflate a validation number without improving anything.

Shared reports. Some reports are byte-identical across studies — a template read for an unremarkable knee — so every study in such a group receives the same derived target vector. Splitting a group across the divide scores the model on a target whose source it was trained on. Studies are assigned by a hash of the report text instead, which keeps every duplicate group whole. One fifth is held out, and the split is fixed rather than rotated.

Two references that mean different things. The holdout measures agreement with the derived targets and has enough studies per label to separate a real difference from noise, so it selects both the epoch within a run and the recipe between runs. The annotation check measures agreement with a radiologist's reading of the images, which is what the competition actually scores — but only annotated studies falling inside the holdout can be used, and there are very few. It is reported and never allowed to arbitrate.

One more failure that produces predictions instead of errors

Weights loaded but read through the wrong preprocessing do not raise anything. The submission is well formed, the log says nothing, and the difference is a number no output of the run reveals. Scaling that never happens, or happens twice, is enough on its own and changes no shape anywhere.

So every set of weights carries a fingerprint: the model's output on a fixed synthetic bag generated from a seed rather than read from disk, pushed through the entire forward path — byte scaling, ImageNet normalisation, resize, encoder, slot attention. Any of those differing moves the output by order one; numerics differing between two GPUs move it by about 1e-5. The tolerance sits at 2e-3, between the two. Every member in the final run reported a match within 5e-6.

What this entry is and is not. The submission is a rank mean of 20 members trained off-platform and read back in as a dataset, because nothing requires the scored run to be the run that learned the weights — and training inside the notebook caps the model at what one accelerator fits in the time limit, then spends that time again on every resubmission for a result that does not change. The label extractor and the pipeline decisions above are the parts I would defend; the ensemble is bookkeeping.
